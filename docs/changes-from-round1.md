# Changes from Round 1

This file records where the Round 2 build differs from what was proposed in Round 1, and why.

## The Round 1 plan, in brief (the baseline)

Round 1 proposed Inheritance as a handover system for university student clubs. It would read a club's WhatsApp exports, meeting minutes (.docx/.md) and Trello JSON, and build a ledger of obligations: commitments made to people, and recurring duties with the approvals they need. Every entry would link to the message it came from, anything unsupported would be shown as a gap, and nothing would be shared until the outgoing lead and the faculty advisor had reviewed and approved it. Each role would see only what it is permitted to, enforced by Postgres row-level security.

The plan named five steps (Connect, Reconstruct, Verify, Review, Handover), five trust states (corroborated, sourced, inferred, contradicted, gap), and these Round 2 features: importers for the three formats with an access level per file; an event timeline; commitment detection with a fulfilment check; declared recurring duties; context extraction; exact-quote verification and trust states; a contradiction docket; review, approval and version history; role-filtered views with sealed items; and per-item acknowledgement. Its stack was Next.js, Supabase, Claude behind one adapter with Zod, `pgvector` with local MiniLM embeddings for matching, `whatsapp-chat-parser` and `mammoth`. It also promised a synthetic two-year club generated by an LLM from planted answers, a second club specified by an independent volunteer, interviews with 8 to 12 club office-bearers before Round 2, and a small usability test.

## Promises from Round 1 that were not kept, and why

- **No user interviews were done.** Round 1 said 8 to 12 office-bearers would be asked "What three things went wrong in your first month in the role?" before Round 2, with the answers reported. That did not happen, and no survey data is quoted anywhere in this project. The working hypothesis (most early handover problems are unknown obligations) is still untested by users.
- **No usability test was run.** Round 1 promised a small test comparing raw exports with Inheritance. It was not done. What exists instead is automated: the pipeline is scored against hand-written ground truth, and leaks are tested at three layers.
- **The second club was not specified by an independent volunteer.** The photography club was designed by the developer with Claude Code, deliberately different from the first (other cast, other file shapes), and its ground truth was written before any model saw its files. It is a weaker test than an outside author's club would be.
- **The synthetic data is not LLM-generated** (see Demo mode below): a seeded script renders hand-written planted facts.
- **Embeddings were dropped** (see Retrieval and matching).

## Retrieval and matching

- **Embeddings dropped for Round 2.** Matching mentions across sources uses an alias table, candidate pairs within a date window that share an entity, `pg_trgm` similarity ranking, and a model verdict (same / different / unsure, where unsure is treated as different and surfaced as a possible match). This avoids an embedding provider and vector storage on free tiers, and keeps each match decision explainable.

## Processing model

- **Client-driven job runner.** Vercel Hobby limits how long a serverless function can run, and Supabase Free has no background workers. Long imports are therefore split into small steps stored in a `jobs` table. The browser polls `POST /api/jobs/run-next` and shows progress until all steps are done.

## Access for judges

- **Demo accounts.** Four pre-seeded synthetic accounts (outgoing lead Arun, advisor Dr. Meena, incoming lead Kavya, treasurer Divya) with one-click login and a role switcher. Their credentials are shown on the login page because the data is synthetic. Row-level security still applies to every account, so switching roles shows what each role can really read.

## Schema additions (for leak-safe permissions)

Row-level security filters whole rows, not values inside JSON, so a few additions keep restricted text from leaking:

- **`version_items`** holds one immutable row per obligation per approved version, with its own access level. `versions.snapshot`/`diff` contain only ids and hashes, and each role's diff is built from the items it can read.
- **`releases`** records explicit releases: the outgoing lead releasing a private item, or the advisor releasing an item to the incoming lead. Each release is written through a database function that also writes the audit log.
- **`audit_log.access_level` / `private_uploaders`**: audit rows carry the access level of the row they describe, because they store before/after copies of it.
- **`obligations.private_uploaders`**: who uploaded the private sources behind an obligation, so the outgoing lead can see private items derived only from their own files.

## Permission model details (Phase 1)

- **Automatic level inheritance.** A database trigger raises an obligation to the most restrictive level among the messages it cites, and records who uploaded any private sources behind it. Import code can't accidentally publish a private item at a lower level.
- **Releases share the item, not the conversation.** Releasing a private obligation shows its description and verified quotes to the receiving role, but the full private message stays hidden.
- **Audit visibility follows the item.** Audit rows store the access level of the item they describe. An audit row about an obligation is readable only while the reader can read that obligation. This stops private text from leaking through before/after copies.
- **Workspace deletion.** `delete_workspace()` is the only way to remove immutable rows (versions, acknowledgements, audit log). It deletes the whole workspace at once.
- **Acknowledgements are append-only.** Changing a decision adds a new row, and the latest row per item and user is the current answer (`latest_acks` view).

## Parsers and time (Phase 2)

- **One time zone, IST.** Chat exports have no time zone and clubs are in India, so every timestamp is stored as an IST (`+05:30`) ISO string. Trello's UTC times are converted. This keeps ordering consistent across WhatsApp, Trello and minutes, which matters for fulfilment ("later events") and for the Trello-done-versus-chat contradiction check.
- **Dropped items are counted, not hidden.** System lines, deleted messages and attachments without text do not become events, but the import summary reports how many were dropped and why.
- **Minutes become one event per dated heading.** Quotes are verified against the whole section, with markdown decoration stripped so a quote matches what a reader sees. Word files are read through their heading styles.
- **Trello contributes history, not only cards.** Comments, list moves and completion changes become events, so a card that was moved to Done can later be compared with what people said afterwards.

## Import flow (Phase 3)

- **Files are not stored.** An upload is parsed on the server and only its messages, a counts-only summary and its SHA-256 fingerprint are kept. This avoids a second copy of sensitive chats to protect and delete. It also sets a practical limit of about 3.9 MB per import, from Vercel's request size cap. Supabase Storage can be added later if real exports turn out larger.
- **Imports are atomic and checked by RLS.** `import_source()` runs as the uploader, so the same row-level policies that govern reading also govern writing. A failed import leaves nothing behind.
- **Sensible defaults for access.** One-to-one chats (two people) default to private, as the brief requires. Files whose names mention money default to finance. The uploader can change either before importing.
- **The shared demo club is protected.** Visitors cannot delete it; only the seed script can reset it. Per-visitor workspaces arrive with "Load demo club".

## Language model use (Phase 4)

- **Structured outputs instead of forced tool use.** The brief asked for tool use as the structured-output mechanism. Current Claude Sonnet and Opus models reject forced tool calls, so the adapter uses the documented replacement, `output_config.format` with a JSON schema generated from Zod. The guarantee is the same: output must match the schema, and it is validated again with Zod.
- **The quote check runs twice.** It runs once in TypeScript and once inside the database function that stores a commitment. A bug in the application cannot store an unverified claim.
- **Extraction runs as the person who started it.** Every read and write goes through their session and RLS, so the model never receives a message that person cannot read. Job records hold message ids only, never text.
- **A deterministic hedge filter backs up the prompt.** Quotes containing "maybe", "might", "will see", "not sure" and similar are dropped by code even if the model returns them.
- **A daily budget and a response cache.** Repeated runs over the same messages reuse saved answers. A per-day limit on uncached calls protects the hosted demo from runaway cost.

## Fulfilment, recurring duties and context (Phase 5)

- **One run, in stages.** A single "Build the ledger" run finds commitments, then recurring duties, then checks every item for follow-up and context. Jobs are claimed stage by stage (`claim_next_run_job()`), so follow-up checks are planned only once the items exist. Each serverless step is still one small job.
- **Status is computed, not asked for.** The model only points at quotes and says whether each one reports the item done, implies it, or says it is not done. A fixed rule turns verified quotes into a status, and the same rule runs in SQL. A fifth status was added, **Likely done (inferred)**, for evidence that only implies completion, so an inference is never shown as fact. A checked item with nothing verifiable is **No evidence kept**; an unchecked one says "Not yet checked".
- **Fulfilment must come after the promise.** Follow-up evidence dated before the promise, or the promise message itself, is refused by code and again by the database.
- **Code chooses what the model reads.** Related messages for a follow-up check are picked deterministically (rare shared words, same Trello card), not by the model.
- **Prompts never mix access levels.** This was found while building recurring duties: a description written from a committee message and a finance message together could carry finance facts into a committee-level row. Commitment chunks and duty batches now hold one access level each, and one uploader for private files. The database refuses a duty whose quotes span levels. A duty already known from a less restricted record is not stored again at a more restricted one. (Finance readers may still see a near-duplicate when the wording differs; Phase 6 matching handles that.)
- **Follow-up evidence raises visibility (kept by choice).** By the existing provenance rule, an item inherits the most restrictive message it cites. If finance minutes say a committee-level promise was paid, the item becomes finance-level, because its status now comes from a finance record.
- **Context notes carry their own level.** `context_items` now cite a message and a verified quote and inherit that message's level (and uploader, for private files). A note may not name a person or number missing from its own message, and must be about its item.
- **Dates are derived by code and labelled.** Next-due and start-by dates come from a stated month, or one period after the last recorded instance (marked "Estimated"). Otherwise the ledger says the date is not derivable.
- **Standing rules are duties, not commitments.** The extraction prompt now leaves general rules ("a permission letter is needed before every booking") to the duty finder, while a person's promise to do it this time is still a commitment.

## Matching, trust and the docket (Phase 6)

- **Trust is computed by the database, every time.** The trust rule runs inside the obligation trigger, so it follows every change to an item's evidence or links, including evidence that disappears when a file is removed (the item becomes a **gap**). No client, reviewer or model can set it. A checked commitment's status is recomputed the same way, so status and trust always agree.
- **"Corroborated" means independent.** Two messages corroborate an item only if they come from different files or from different named people. One person promising and then reporting it done in the same chat stays **sourced**. An unknown author never counts as a second person, and an inferred quote never corroborates.
- **Linked, never merged.** Items recorded twice (a chat promise and its Trello card; a duty in the chat and again in the minutes) are linked as the same item, or as a possible match when the model is unsure. Each record keeps its own access level and quotes.
- **Evidence flows only downwards.** An item may use the evidence of a linked item only if everyone who can read it can also read the other: a less restricted level, or the same level (and, for private items, files from the same uploaders). A finance record can make the finance copy contradicted, but never changes what the committee copy tells the incoming lead.
- **Code picks the pairs; the model only gives a verdict.** Pairs are scored on wording (trigram similarity as `pg_trgm` computes it, and shared words), the people they are owed to or approved by (through the alias table), and a 60-day window for commitments. Near-identical pairs with matching people and numbers link by rule. The uncertain middle goes to the model in batches of eight. A match prompt may show two items of different levels side by side (both readable by the reviewer), because the model returns only a verdict and writes nothing that is stored.
- **The docket fills itself.** A contradicted item opens a docket entry with its latest "done" claim and its latest "not done" claim, both quotes, and a summary written by code from the two messages' file type and date. An entry is visible only to someone who can read both messages and the item. It closes by itself when the conflict goes, unless someone has already ruled on it. Rulings arrive with review (Phase 8).
- **Model-written descriptions are checked too.** A commitment's description may not name a person or number missing from its message; if it does, the verified quote is shown instead.

## Ledger UI and evidence pane (Phase 7)

- **Every quote opens its source.** "Open source message" shows the full message with the quote highlighted and up to two messages either side, in IST. The highlight uses the verifier's own normalisation, mapped back onto the original text; if a quote cannot be placed, the message is shown unhighlighted and says so. The page reads through RLS: a quote shared with a role that cannot read the message says so, and a quote the role cannot read at all is "not available to your role".
- **One card per item, with its other records inside.** A promise recorded in the chat and again in Trello or the minutes is one card; each other record is nested under "Also in Trello" with its own access level, trust label and quotes. Grouping uses direct links only (links are not transitive), and possible matches are a quiet note that is never merged or used as evidence.
- **Conflicts link both ways.** A contradicted item links to its Docket entry, and the entry links back to the item.
- **The Gaps tab is live.** It lists items with no kept evidence, promises with no follow-up, promises not yet checked, and duties with no derivable next date, each with what would close the gap. Only items the role may read count, so a gap is a gap in what that role can see.
- **One trust legend** replaces the repeated sentence on each card. Contradicted cards keep their own one-line explanation.
- **Messages keep their file order.** Events now carry `seq`, their position in the file, so the evidence pane shows the messages around a quote in the order they were written, even when many share one minute (WhatsApp) or one date (minutes).
- **Folded records keep their facts.** A record folded into another card still shows who owes whom and when (or how often and the next date), with any disagreement with the card marked. Links that cannot be folded are listed, so none is hidden.
- No AI use changed in this phase.

## Review, sign-off and versions (Phase 8)

- **Two-step sign-off.** The outgoing lead checks each item against its quotes (accept, edit, reject, move back to draft) and marks the accepted draft "ready for the advisor". Only the faculty advisor can approve, and only while that mark still matches the pending items: any later change to an accepted item or a note means it must be marked ready again. The advisor's own rulings do not invalidate the mark.
- **Every change needs a reason.** A signed-in user can no longer update an item directly. Changes go through `review_obligation()`, which needs a reason, limits edits to a fixed list of fields per kind (status, trust and access level are never editable), and writes an audit row named for the action (`accept`, `edit`, `reject`, `reopen`).
- **Items remember what they were accepted with.** If a later run changes the status or trust of an accepted item, it is flagged "Changed since accepted: was X, now Y". Marking ready and approving are both refused until each flagged item is accepted again or rejected.
- **Rulings are recorded, not rewritten.** The advisor rules on a conflict (done, not done, cannot tell) with a reason. The ruling is shown as the advisor's decision and travels in the version, but the item stays contradicted, because the records still disagree. Status still comes from evidence alone.
- **"Not the same" is remembered.** A reviewer's verdict on a pair (`same` or `distinct`, method `reviewer`) stands: matching skips that pair and never asks the model about it again, and a `distinct` pair never lends evidence.
- **Versions hold more.** Format 2 items also carry the `inferred` flag on evidence, the same-item links the item may borrow from, and the conflicts with their rulings. Context notes sit in their own immutable table (`version_context_items`) so each keeps its own access level: a finance note never rides inside a committee item. Hashes of earlier versions are never rewritten; the next diff compares an older item on the fields it recorded.
- **The working ledger is not for the incoming lead.** Until a version is approved, only the outgoing lead, the advisor and the treasurer read obligations (and so evidence, notes, links and conflicts). The incoming lead and members read approved versions only, filtered by the same access rules, so a release still reaches the incoming lead through a version. Messages and files (the timeline) are unchanged.
- **Version diffs are built from rows the role can read.** The diff shown to each role is computed from that role's readable items, not from a stored diff, so it cannot show a restricted item or field.
- No AI use changed in this phase.

## Handover (Phase 9)

- **`/handover` reads the latest approved version only**, as the viewer's role may read it (`version_items` through row-level security). It is the incoming lead's page: "People waiting on you" (open commitments grouped by who they are owed to, soonest first, with a last group for items that name no one), a calendar of recurring duties by next due date with "Overdue" and "Start now" markers, and sealed placeholders. Items with no evidence say "No evidence kept". Reviewers see the same page with the incoming lead's answers.
- **Sealed placeholders** come from `sealed_items` (holder and a short topic, no source text). The outgoing lead or advisor can add one for a finance or private item that has not been released; the topic is shown to every member, so the form says to leave details out.
- **Acknowledgements** (accept, ask a question, decline) are immutable rows; the latest per item wins. Only the incoming lead can answer, and only an item they can read. A question or a decline needs a note. Each answer now writes an audit row (migration 0016) with the item's access level, so the audit is filtered like the item.
- No AI use changed in this phase; no paid model calls were made.

## Demo mode (Phase 10)

- **Each visitor gets their own demo club.** "Load demo club" on the sign-in page builds a private copy of the synthetic club: a workspace with four accounts of its own (outgoing lead, advisor, incoming lead, treasurer), the same messages and items, and version 1 approved. One visitor's reviews, approvals and answers never reach another, and row-level security works in a copy exactly as in a real club. The role menu switches only between the accounts of the visitor's own copy.
- **No stored passwords for the copies.** Each copy account's password is derived on the server (an HMAC of its email under the server's secret key), so the server can switch roles without keeping credentials. A signed, http-only cookie lets a visitor who signed out return to their copy; it grants nothing after the copy expires.
- **Copies expire.** A copy and its accounts are deleted after 7 days by a daily job, or earlier when the visitor deletes it (from Connect) or starts a fresh one. At most 300 copies can be active and 60 started per hour, so the free database cannot be flooded.
- **The shared accounts remain**, below the "Load demo club" card, for anyone who wants them. They are labelled as shared.
- **A daily keep-alive.** Supabase Free pauses a project after a week without activity, so a Vercel cron (once a day, the Hobby limit) queries the database and runs the clean-up. It needs `CRON_SECRET`; without it the route refuses every caller.
- No AI use changed in this phase.
- **Synthetic data is generated by a seeded script, not by a model.** The brief described an LLM generator that renders planted facts as chat. I built the planted facts and the ground truth first, in YAML, and a deterministic renderer for the filler. It is free, repeatable and judged against fixed truth. An LLM renderer can be added later behind the adapter.

## Scoring, leak tests and a second club (Phase 12)

- **The pipeline is scored against fixed truth.** `pnpm eval:pipeline -- --scenario <name>` grades a run on any scenario: commitments found and kept wrongly, status, trust and access level, follow-up quotes, contradictions, duties and approvers, wrong links, and leaks. Truth is written by hand before any model sees the files, and lists accepted alternate wordings explicitly (a promise stated twice in the same minutes) rather than matching loosely.
- **A second synthetic club, built to differ.** A photography club with a different cast, an iPhone committee chat, a finance chat, the outgoing lead's private chat with the advisor, Word minutes with dates in words, and no Trello. The generator now writes one-to-one chats and `.docx` files (a small zip writer, no new dependency). The year club gained the advisor's private chat.
- **Leaks are tested at three layers.** In memory: nothing the model writes is stored at a more open level than a restricted fact it carries, and no prompt whose answer is stored mixes access levels. In the database: after a full run and a signed-off version, every row of every table, view and read function is searched, per role, for finance amounts and private subjects that role may not read. In the browser: every page and every source-message URL (taken from the advisor's view and opened directly by every role) is searched the same way. Each check also proves the restricted text is visible to someone allowed, so it cannot pass by reading nothing.
- **Fixed, because the tests found them:**
  - The outgoing lead's own private chat produced no items when he built the ledger: a new private item had no uploaders until its first quote, so the row was unreadable to him and refused. Migration 0018 carries the uploader from the cited file.
  - Extraction made one model call per chat-day when two chats interleave (the database reads in time order), 110 calls instead of 13 for the photo club. Chunks are now packed per file group; they still never mix access levels.
  - The hedge filter checked only the quote, so "Maybe I'll shoot the convocation too" passed when the quote left out "Maybe". It now checks the quote's whole sentence, and knows Tamil-English hedges ("paarkalaam", "theriyala").
  - "Mr." and "Prof." ended sentences, cutting quotes at "Mr. Dsouza".
  - The matching rule linked two semesters' identical promises (the permission letter by 14 November, and again by 31 January), so each borrowed the other's evidence. Different due dates now send the pair to the model.
- **Not done:** loading the year club into visitors' demo copies (Phase 13).

## Hardening, demo choices and replies (Phase 13)

- **An implied completion can no longer hide an item (P6).** A follow-up quote that only *implies* a promise was kept, from a record more restricted than the promise (a finance chat, say), is now dropped and counted. Before, by the provenance rule, one weak hint raised a committee item to finance level and hid it from the incoming lead. Quotes that say plainly "done" or "not done" still raise the level, as decided in Phase 5. Checked in TypeScript and again in `record_followup()` (migration 0019).
- **A Trello card is a commitment only with a person or a date.** Card descriptions such as "Count what the lab has." were kept as promises and cost most of the year club's precision. A card now counts only if it names who is waiting for it or carries a due date. Precision on the year club rose from 79% to 83% with every planted item still found.
- **Visitors choose what their demo club starts with.** "Explore a built handover" loads a year of the synthetic IoT club (1,961 messages in five files at four levels) with the ledger a real model built from it, frozen once and loaded without a model call, version 1 approved and the model's six extra items left as drafts to review. "Start from your own files" gives the same four people and nothing else, and Connect offers the photography club's four sample files with the level to set for each. Round 1 described one demo club; two paths let a judge either inspect a full result quickly or run the whole flow from files.
- **Questions go back to the outgoing team.** Round 1's workflow said the incoming lead's questions "go back to Arun", but until now he could only read them. The outgoing lead and the advisor can now reply under a question or a decline. Replies are append-only, readable exactly when the question is, and audited (migration 0020).
- **Verify is a step of its own in the header.** It opens the Docket; the Docket, Gaps and source-message pages mark it as the current step.
- **A walkthrough page** (`/guide`) explained both demo paths. Removed in Phase 14.
- **Saved answers can be pre-loaded.** `pnpm cache:warm` copies the model's answers for the synthetic scenarios to the hosted cache, so building from the sample files returns at once. Pre-loaded rows do not count against the daily call budget.

## Guided demo tour (after Phase 13; removed in Phase 14)

- **Loading a demo club now starts a guided tour.** A built club used to land on the home page with everything already done (imported, built, version 1 approved), so a first-time visitor jumped straight into a finished ledger and never saw what each step did. A panel docked at the bottom of every page now goes through Connect, Reconstruct, Verify, Review and Handover in order: what the step does, what to look at, and something to try. It rings the element it is talking about, and when a stop is seen by someone else (Dr. Meena approving, Kavya receiving) its Next button signs in as that person, through the same role switch as the menu.
- **Two tracks:** "Explore a built handover" (18 stops) and "Build one from your own files" (12 stops). Either can be started or restarted from the Walkthrough page, hidden, left and returned to. The tour only explains and navigates; it changes no data, and every page it opens is read under the signed-in role's RLS.

## Round 3: direction (decided 14 Nov 2026)

- **The guided tour and the `/guide` walkthrough page were removed in Phase 14.** The Round 3 brief says a judge must complete the main journey without a walkthrough, so the pages themselves say what each is for and what to do next.
- **Order:** UI/UX first (Phase 14); then the three improvements committed in the Round 2 report, which are gap questions (15), counterparty handoff notes (16) and inferred recurring duties (17); then hardening and documentation (18). Every phase is merged and deployed.
- **No demo video.** The Round 2 plan listed one for Round 3; the Round 3 brief rules it out.

## Round 3, Phase 14: the app guides itself

- **Home page shows each person their next step.** Signed in, it names the one thing to do (add files, build the ledger, check items, mark ready, approve, answer items), with one main button and a progress bar worked out from the data the person can read. When the next step belongs to someone else, the button switches to that person.
- **Every page says in one line what it is for,** and the step bar is plain language and always visible (numbers only on a phone's home page).
- **A new look:** pastel purple, rounded cards, larger buttons, visible focus rings, soft badges.
- **Waiting no longer feels frozen:** skeleton placeholders on every page, a bar under the header on navigation, an animated progress bar while the ledger builds and while a demo club is made, and a "Switching to …" cover while a person switch signs in. All motion stops for visitors who ask for reduced motion.
- **Mistakes are harder:** buttons disable and show a spinner while they work, removing a file and deleting a club ask first, and an Enter key cannot skip the question.
- **Empty and error states** now say what is missing and what to do, instead of a bare line or a blank.
- **Plain-language names:** Promises, Yearly duties, Conflicts, All messages, Check and sign off, Rulings, Approved versions, Audit history.
- **One page per step.** Conflicts and Gaps moved to their own page, Check the records (`/check`); the full message list moved to All messages (`/messages`, linked from the header). Tabs inside a page no longer move the step bar, and every step page ends with a button to the next one, in order: Add files → Build the ledger → Check the records → Review and approve → Hand over.
- **Building the ledger shows progress the moment it is pressed,** then which part is being read and roughly how long is left.
- **Adding files is three numbered parts,** with a one-click "Use the 4 sample files" (the download links stay), the authorisation box next to the button, and a line under the button saying what it still needs.

## Round 3, Phase 15: gap questions for the outgoing lead (improvement 2)

Committed in the Round 2 report (section 10) as improvement 2.

- **One page of questions,** the Questions tab of Check the records (`/check?tab=questions`), for every gap the records cannot close: a promise with no follow-up ("Was this done?"), an unruled conflict ("The records disagree. Was it done?"), a duty with no approver, and a duty with no next date. Each can be answered, or answered "I don't know", which leaves it knowingly open and can be changed later. The home page sends the outgoing lead here before review.
- **An answer is a record, not an edit.** It is saved as a message from the outgoing lead, in a file labelled "Stated by the outgoing lead" (one per access level), and that message is cited as the item's evidence: done, not done, an approver, "no approval needed" or a date. "I don't know" becomes a note on the item. So the quote check, the trust and status rules, the access level, the audit log, re-review, the sign-off and the version diff all apply as they do to any other record. The answer is never presented as anything but the lead's own word: every quote from such a file is labelled, on the ledger, the review, the approved version, its diff and the handover.
- **The lead chooses who may read each answer,** never less restricted than the item (the message names the item). A more restricted answer raises the item, and the form says who would stop seeing it before it is saved.
- **The form shows the exact message before it is saved.** The message is written by code from the chosen answer and the item's description, followed by the lead's own words if any.
- **Only the outgoing lead answers.** The advisor and the treasurer see the questions and answers (they read the working ledger); the incoming lead and members see answers only in approved versions. A conflict answer is shown to the advisor next to the two disagreeing records; the advisor still rules.
- **The incoming lead sees the lead's words on the card itself,** with a note that they are the lead's own word. An answer reaches her only in the next approved version.
- **Conflicts are explained to the incoming lead:** a contradicted item says the records disagree, shows both sides, and gives the advisor's ruling and reason, or says that nobody had ruled when the version was approved.
- **Promises already done now appear on the handover** under "Already done", so the incoming lead can read and answer every item in a version (before, fulfilled promises were counted but not shown).
- **No model is involved,** and the pipeline never reads the lead's answers as new records.

## Round 3, Phase 16: counterparty handoff notes (improvement 3)

Committed in the Round 2 report (section 10) as improvement 3.

- **One note per person or office outside the club** that the latest approved version names: a promise's counterparty (a sponsor, a lab in-charge, speakers, volunteers), or the approver of a recurring duty (the HoD, the Dean's office). The club itself and its committees are not counterparties. Found by code; records that name them differently ("all workshop speakers", "workshop speakers") are one counterparty.
- **Built only from approved items the incoming lead may read** (level everyone or committee, never finance or private), chosen and copied by the database from the approved version, so a note cannot carry restricted text. A promise and its Trello card, marked as the same item, are said once.
- **A fixed template, no model text:** what the club still owes them, by when; what the club asks of them each time (for a duty they approve); what is already done; and that the incoming lead is their contact now. It is honest about what the records do not settle: "Our records do not show this as done yet", "Our records disagree on whether this was done; Kavya will check with you".
- **The outgoing lead edits and approves it** on Notes to people outside the club (`/handover/notes`): who it is to, the new contact, how to reach them, a short message in their own words, and which items go in, with a live preview of the exact note. Only the outgoing lead can draft, edit, approve or discard a note. An approved note never changes. A draft saved before a newer version was approved must be saved again before approval.
- **A signed link** (`/n/…`): the counterparty opens the note with no account. The server checks the link's signature (an HMAC under a server-only key) before reading anything, then reads that one note through a function only the server may call. The page shows none of the app's own header, and pressing "I have read this note" (with an optional name) records an acknowledgement once.
- **The acknowledgement shows on the handover,** in a new "Notes to people outside the club" section, for the outgoing lead and the incoming lead (who sees which notes name her as the contact), and in the review History, as coming from the signed link.
- **Guidance:** once a version is approved, the home page sends the outgoing lead to write the notes before handing on to the incoming lead, and offers a note beside the gap questions and the review.
- Every draft, edit, approval, discard and acknowledgement writes an audit row with before and after, at the note's level.
